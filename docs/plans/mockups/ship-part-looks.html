How should machines look on the hull?

Four treatments of the same ship, the Halvex Runner (12×7 cells, composite hull). Armour stays a flat tile in every option; only the machines change. Pick a letter, or mix: for example C everywhere and D only for engines.

Today

Reference. Every machine is a dark block with an icon on it; engines read as a square with a bell symbol.

B

Detailed tiles

Tiles stay square, icons stay; each tile gets a cue of its machine cut in.

Smallest step. Nozzle mouths, vents and recesses darken parts of the tile, but a thruster is still a square block.

C

Housings

The machine is drawn as its own shape, sitting in a hull-coloured cell.

Engines become nozzle bells, reactors round housings, radiators fin stacks. The drawing replaces the icon; the system colour stays as its accent.

D

Housings + silhouette

Like C, and the working ends reach past the hull.

Nozzle bells stick out at the stern, longer barrels at the nose, radiator fins over the side. The outline tells you what the ship does.

What each option means for the game

Style rules
Style 2a (GDR-0007) rejected bevels, gradients, rounded or gapped tiles and smooth hulls. All four options keep flat fills, seams and square armour. C and D drop the rule "one flat colour per machine tile with its icon on it": the machine becomes a shape on a hull-coloured cell. Choosing C or D means a new record that replaces that one rule.
Reading a ship
A and B identify parts by icon. C and D identify them by shape, keep the system colour as an accent, and are easier to read when zoomed out, because a nozzle bell or a round reactor is recognisable at a few pixels.
Collisions
In D the parts that reach past the hull (nozzles up to 0.35 cells, fins 0.3, barrels as today) are drawn only; collisions stay on the grid cells. Small enough that a hit next to a nozzle still looks right.
Work
B is a small change to the icon SVGs. C and D need one new vector drawing per part, plus the hull-coloured cell under machines (the hull material of the neighbours, or the design's main material). D also needs the outward direction of each part, which the drydock already knows for guns, engines and RCS pods.
Recommendation
D: it fixes what feels off (engines that are squares) and makes the outline say what a ship does, while armour keeps the block look.
