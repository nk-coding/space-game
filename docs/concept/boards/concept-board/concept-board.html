Program

How ships think

In v1, scripts drove the ship directly. That was tedious, and one bad value sent the ship spinning. Here behavior is layered: you pick goals, and the layers underneath work out how to reach them safely.

Behavior treeYou edit this

A priority list of conditions and actions, like Gladiabots. Presets such as Escort, Hunter, Miner and Guard are trees you can open and change.

ManeuversBuilt in, set by parameters

Approach, orbit, keep range, strafe, flank, dock, hold formation slot, flee. Advanced players can script new ones.

Flight computerAlways on

Turns intent into thruster output, enforces speed and spin limits, avoids collisions. Nothing above it can push the ship past those limits.

Goals flow down. Only the flight computer fires thrusters.

Conditions can only read what the ship's sensors detect. Rule slots come from logic cores, so a cheap drone runs a short tree and a capital ship a long one.

Behavior as text

Trees get a small text format of their own: the same tree as the node editor, one line per rule. You can switch between nodes and text at any time.

Custom nodes need a real language. Whichever one it is, a script only returns a goal for the flight computer, runs sandboxed with an instruction budget, and is safe to share. The tabs show the same flank maneuver in each candidate.

tree "Escort drone"
  when shield < 30%                  -> retreat_to leader
  when missile_incoming, pd_ready    -> point_defense nearest_missile
  when enemy_attacks leader          -> flank attacker, side: left
  when wreck_in_range, no_enemies    -> tractor nearest_wreck
  always                             -> hold_slot left_wing
// custom node, appears in the tree editor as "flank"
fn flank(ship, target, p) {
    let side = if p.side == "left" { -1.0 } else { 1.0 };
    let goal = target.pos + target.right * side * p.dist;
    go_to(goal, #{ face: target })
}

Suggestion: tree text for everyone. For custom nodes, pick the language together with the engine: Rhai if it's Rust, pocketpy if it's C++ or Godot. Both read cleanly and need no end. With the all-Rust stack below, that means Rhai, which also powers mods.

Fly · controls

Flying with thumbs

Stick + lock is the pick for Android. Without a lock, the stick means “fly that way”: the flight computer turns the nose and thrusts, and how far you push sets the speed. Letting go brakes to a stop with the ship’s own thrusters; a drift toggle switches braking off. Top speed is a soft cap that comes from thrust and mass. On PC, WASD is the stick and the mouse locks and fires. The other two schemes stay here for reference.

Fight

Combat feel

Real-time, physical and readable. Small ships dart, big ships lumber, under the same physics, with the overall feel leaning fast. Fights happen at mid range, one to two screens apart, with smooth zoom.

Who aimsYou aim the fixed guns by turning, with target lock helping. Turrets aim through the behavior tree, and you set their priorities: engines first, weapons first, nearest threat.
Where shots landOuter blocks take hits first. Lasers burn the first block, railguns punch through several, missiles splash. Slopes deflect kinetic shots at shallow angles. Destroyed reactors, capacitors and drives explode into their neighbours.
ShieldsEach emitter projects its own bubble over nearby blocks. Bubbles stack where they overlap and drop when the emitter loses power.
RammingCollisions deal damage by mass and speed, so a reinforced prow and a big engine make a weapon too.
Heat and overdriveOverheated parts shut down by default. Overdrive overrides that for burst output, at the risk of damage or an explosion.
Repairs mid-fightRepair drones fly out and patch blocks using repair material from the hold. They can be shot down.
CaptureKill all of a ship's power and its logic core goes dark. Copy yourself into it and the ship is yours.
Sensors, HUD, timeSensors set targeting range and scan detail; nearby ships are always visible. The HUD stays minimal: damage shows on the ships themselves, plus a hull map of your own ship. Always real-time, no pause.

How each race fights

Rogue automatonsSmall packs

Simple ships in groups of three to five. They rush, flank badly and teach you to use your turrets.

The GrownClose and stubborn

They regrow damage, so focused fire matters. They love ramming with dense living prows.

The SwarmFloods

Clouds of drones from hive carriers. Point defense and splash weapons pay off; kill the carrier and the drones scatter.

The LatticeLong-range

Snipers with prism beams and crystal armor that refracts lasers. Close the distance or bring kinetics.

Phase walkersBlink strikes

They blink in, hit through armor with phase lances, and blink out. Shields and fast turrets matter more than plating.

GuardiansBreakable machines

Huge precursor machines taken apart piece by piece; each section you destroy changes how the rest fights.

Progress

Tech trees

No Mk levels. Every system family has its own tree, around 200 nodes in total. Most nodes are trade-offs, and every new part or variant gets its own silhouette, so a ship's tech shows on its hull.

  • New partNew silhouette, new role
  • VariantSidegrade with a trade-off
  • Size classBigger footprint for bigger hulls
  • RefinementShort I–V chain of small stat steps
  • AlienNeeds reverse-engineered tech

Example: energy weapons

Where research comes fromDismantling salvaged parts in the drydock gives research data for that part's family. Alien nodes also need an artifact or lore from that race's derelicts.
Cross-linksSome nodes need progress in another tree: cooling jackets need Thermal, overcharge needs Power. Specialists still have to branch out a little.
Data, not codeEvery node and number lives in data files, so the balance arena can test the whole tree without a rebuild.

World

The galaxy

You are a ship-mind that wakes up in an abandoned precursor shipyard at the rim. That shipyard becomes home. The precursor archive waits at the core, about 50 hours of play away. In between: star clusters linked by lanes, four alien races, and precursor gates that only open once their guardians fall.

The voyage loop

  1. Refit at the shipyard
  2. Gate to the frontier
  3. Cruise and warp between clusters
  4. Fight, salvage, scan
  5. Head home with the haul
  6. Research, then refit
Short by defaultA normal voyage covers one cluster and takes 15–30 minutes, so it fits a phone session. Longer voyage types exist (deep expeditions, boss hunts, megastructure dives), but nothing forces them.
Inside a clusterClusters are big, 10+ minutes to cross. You fight patrols and defenders, salvage and scan, and take light missions from race stations. Hazards come with the band.
TravelCruise inside a cluster, warp along lanes to nearby clusters, and use precursor gates for the long distances once their guardians are beaten.
SuppliesAmmo, fuel and repair material run down during a voyage. Home refills everything; outposts refill supplies and handle heavy repairs.
SalvageLoose debris is picked up by flying through it or with a tractor beam; intact parts on big wrecks have to be cut free. Cargo bays are ship parts, so a salvage run trades firepower for hold space.
ScanningScans give research data (it counts once home, like salvage), reveal the cluster map for later voyages, unlock lore, and show an enemy's parts and behavior tree before you engage.
The haulSalvage only becomes research once it reaches the shipyard. Dying sends you home with your ship, but part of the haul stays behind as a wreck.
A static world that refillsClusters keep their content. Cleared encounters come back slowly over time. Races mostly stay in their regions, so clashes between them are rare, except for the Swarm, which raids its neighbours.

Home: the shipyard

The first hour, and how the shipyard grows with you.

  1. Wake as a tiny drone in a dead shipyard
  2. Salvage automaton wrecks nearby
  3. Repair the drydock

Research lab, fabricator, test rangeThe lab turns the haul into research. The fabricator builds parts and escorts and resolves material chains by itself; its upgrades unlock new processing, bigger and alien parts. The test range runs simulated fights against archived enemies and your own past designs.
Bigger shipsTwo limits grow together: drydock expansions enlarge the build grid (frigate, cruiser and capital frames), and your mind capacity sets how many parts you can control. Logic cores and research raise it.
Bonded matterParts are made of precursor programmable matter. Building a part consumes its materials once, bonding its matter to your mind. When a ship is destroyed, its bonded matter is recalled through the gates and re-forms at home, so rebuilding is free. The haul isn't bonded yet, which is why it stays behind as a wreck. Removing a part during a refit gives its matter back.
GuardiansHuge precursor machines built from parts, like your own ships. You take them apart piece by piece, and what falls off is precursor tech. Every guardian can be beaten solo.
AlliesOnly inside specific race missions. Outside of those, the fleet is yours alone.
Room to growAbout 80 clusters for now. Cluster count, band sizes, races and set pieces are generator parameters and data, and saves must tolerate new clusters and bands being added later.

Races

Races exist mainly for variety and tech: each one teaches its own branch. Access, allies and missions exist but stay light, and so does the story.

Rogue automatonsRim and derelicts · hostile

Precursor defense drones gone feral. Plain block ships, simple behavior. They teach the basics and drop plenty of salvage.

The GrownOuter reach · wary

Organic hulls that regrow, bio-reactors, living armor. Leave their living wrecks alone and they leave you alone. Boss: giant Grown ships.

The SwarmMiddle reach · hostile by nature

No standing, no talks. Hive motherships launch endless drones. Their tech only comes from salvage: drones, hangars, swarm logic. Boss: swarm hives.

The LatticeInner reach · neutral, open to standing

Crystalline hex-grid builders. Crystal armor refracts energy weapons; prism arrays split beams. The most likely allies.

Phase walkersInner reach, toward the core · wary

Phase tech that ignores armor, blink drives, ships that flicker between states. Boss: giant phase ships.

PrecursorsGone

Built the gates, the guardians, the megastructures, and you. Their archive at the core holds the best tech in the game.

Your ship comes firstThe fleet is secondary. Escorts are built at home within your command capacity, drones launch from hangar parts, and a disabled enemy ship can be captured by copying yourself into its logic core.
Enemy designsHandcrafted per race, plus designs evolved offline in each race's style and curated into the game. At runtime they get variation (swapped parts within a role, loadouts, damage), not full generation.
FriendsCo-op voyages in the host's galaxy. Every boss can be beaten solo with a good design; friends make it easier. Arena PvP with the ships you actually built.
After the coreThe archive is the goal of the journey, not the end of the game: bigger ships, boss hunts and the arena keep going. New game+ is decided later.
Salvage · fabrication

Materials and fabrication

No generic scrap. Every material comes from a specific place, so what you can build depends on where you've been. The shipyard's fabricator turns raw materials into parts by itself.

HarvestAsteroids, comets, gas pockets, star skimming, with tools like mining lasers, tractors and gas scoops.
SalvageDismantling a part gives research data and its materials back. Or install it as-is; bonding it costs a little programmable matter.
RacesGrown tissue and heartwood, Swarm chitin and nerve cores, Lattice crystal, Phase walker residue and rift silt.
Precursor sitesAutomaton cores, guardians and megastructures: precursor alloy, programmable matter, gate shards, archive keys.
MissionsRace missions reward their rarest materials, like heartwood or resonant crystal.
Chains without factoriesQueue a part and the fabricator works out the chain: ore becomes steel, steel and fissiles become fuel rods, and so on. No buildings to place, no logistics. Conversion happens automatically as long as the raw materials are in storage and the hub has the right upgrades.
Hub upgradesSmelter, kiln, cryo plant, electronics bench, magnetics lab, fuel works, munitions bay, fusion workshop. Race tech adds a bio-vat, hive press, crystal annealer and phase chamber; the precursor forge comes last.
Materials follow the hazardsHelium-3 needs heat shielding to skim, ionized gas comes out of ion storms, hot isotopes from radiation zones. Environmental gates also gate materials.
You are precursor matterEvery logic core needs programmable matter, since it hosts a copy of you. The automatons at the rim are the first source.
Harvest toolsMining lasers, gas scoops and tractor beams are ship parts, so a mining ship gives up firepower. Harvest drones come later and work while you fly or fight.
Unlimited storage, nothing runs outEverything that reaches the shipyard is kept; the cargo hold on the voyage is the only limit. Every source refills slowly, rare ones included.
Fabrication at home onlyVoyages are for gathering. On the road, repair material only patches damage; new parts are built at the shipyard.

Look

Art direction

Flat vector tiles with a soft glow where the energy is. Ships are built from distinct flat blocks separated by thin dark seams, with no bevels. Every machine tile is one flat colour with its glowing icon sitting right on it. A faint teal glow traces the hull edge; enemies use the same tiles with a coral edge. The drydock and flight show the identical tiles. Tech shows through new parts, sizes and materials.

Every color means one thing

  • VoidSpace. Dark blue-black, never pure black.
  • SteelStructure, armor, neutral UI
  • AmberPower: reactors, capacitors
  • CoralHeat, damage, danger, enemy hull glow
  • CyanWeapons and targeting
  • BlueShields
  • TealPropulsion, your hull glow, “all good”
  • VioletLogic, behaviors, alien tech

Do

  • Flat blocks with thin dark seams, so every block stays readable
  • One flat colour per machine tile, icon right on it
  • Detail that carries information: part, size, material, heat, damage
  • Glow on reactors, engines, weapons, shields and a faint hull edge
  • Motion with a reason: exhaust particles, debris, shield ripples
  • Each faction gets its own shape language and edge colour

Avoid

  • 3D bevels and per-block gradients
  • Dark inner squares behind icons
  • Smoothing the hull until the blocks disappear
  • Glow on everything, bloom as a default
  • Random greebles and mixed detail levels
  • Saturated rainbow neon

Development

Balancing with AI vs AI

Two hundred nodes cannot be balanced by hand. During development, a headless arena runs the real simulation thousands of times and reports what wins, what nobody uses, and why.

  1. Generate designsHand-made archetypes (brick, kiter, glass cannon, carrier) plus evolutionary search over layouts and behavior trees, all within a build-point budget.
  2. Fight headlessSame simulation code as the game, no graphics, many times real time, on every CPU core. Fixed seeds, so any fight replays in the game client.
  3. RateElo or TrueSkill per design across a scenario library: duel, 3 vs 3, swarm vs capital, asteroid field, nebula.
  4. ReportPick rate among top designs, win rate when used, parts that never show up.
  5. Adjust dataChange numbers or tree nodes and rerun. A short run in CI checks every data change.

Evolution as a stress test

Evolution doesn't respect intentions. It finds whatever the rules allow, which makes it the best tester for a system this open.

GenomeThe part list, optionally mirror-locked, plus the behavior tree.
Mutation and crossoverAdd, remove, move or swap parts, change materials. Reorder rules, nudge thresholds, swap subtrees. Crossover splices hull sections and subtrees.
Fitness from fightsWin rate against a rotating pool of past champions and hand-made archetypes, so no design overfits one opponent.
Keep diversityMAP-Elites keeps the best design per niche on a grid of traits such as speed × range. Empty niches show what the rules make unviable.
Tune the numbers tooAn outer loop nudges part stats to grow the number of viable niches and shrink dominant ones. You review its proposals.
Hunt exploitsRun without limits (no budget, odd scenarios) to find ramming, spinning, recoil tricks and AI loopholes. Each find becomes a regression scenario.

    Under the hood

    Tech stack and architecture

    All Rust, Bevy client, no C++, no Godot. The simulation is an engine-free library that runs the same way on every platform, bit for bit, including in the browser.

    • Android mid-range 2021+, Windows, Linux, web
    • 60 fps, 30 Hz simulation
    • Cross-platform determinism
    • Host-authoritative co-op and arena
    • Mods from day one
    • Solo developer with AI help

    Crates

    simShips, parts, physics, heat, power, damage, behavior trees, Rhai scripts. Fixed 30 Hz step, seeded, no graphics, no engine.
    ↙↓↘
    gameBevy client: rendering at 60 fps with interpolation, UI, touch and mouse input, audio.
    netHost-authoritative sessions, join codes, a tiny relay for NAT traversal.
    arenaHeadless fights, evolution, balance reports, determinism checks.
    dataParts, tech trees, materials, races, set pieces and strings as RON files. The base game is loaded exactly like a mod.
    Determinism rulesFixed 30 Hz step. Seeded random streams per system. No hash-map iteration in the simulation, only ordered containers. Software math for trigonometry instead of platform functions. No wall-clock time. Physics engine in its cross-platform deterministic mode. A state hash every tick in debug builds.
    NetcodeThe host simulates; clients receive snapshots, interpolate, and predict their own ship's movement. Determinism isn't required for this, but it gives desync detection, exact replays and a later path to rollback in the arena. The transport must work in browsers too: WebRTC data channels, with WebSockets as a fallback, and the relay doubles as the signalling server.
    ModsA mods folder with RON data and Rhai scripts, using the same loader as the base game. Schemas are versioned and validated. Scripts only see a deterministic API. In co-op, the host's mod list and its hash must match. In the browser, mods load from a zip the player picks or from a URL.
    Content authoringHand-edited RON files for set pieces, races, recipes and trees. Enemy and guardian ships come from the drydock's export. Hot reload during development.
    SavesVersioned saves with migrations. A save stores the galaxy seed, the generator version and the changes, so new clusters and bands can be added later. Export and import by file or share code now; optional cloud sync later. In the browser, saves live in browser storage (IndexedDB) and export the same way.
    Web buildThe same Rust code compiled to WebAssembly and served as static files. A full platform: single player, co-op, mods and saves all work in the browser. WebGL2 everywhere, WebGPU where available. Browser builds probably run on one thread, so the web gets its own performance bar, and the drydock's device warning knows about it. Audio starts after the first tap or click.
    Development setupDevelopment happens in a Linux VM without GPU passthrough. The simulation, tests and arena run natively in the VM. You look at the game through the web build, served from the VM to your host browser. Android builds reach the phone over Wi-Fi debugging (adb).
    TextEnglish only at launch, but every string lives in data files under a key, so translations can be added without code changes.
    One space per clusterEach cluster is its own simulation space with small, precise local coordinates. Moving between clusters is a short warp sequence while the next one loads. The galaxy still feels seamless because the map, gates and lanes connect everything.
    What runs whereOnly occupied clusters simulate; empty ones freeze and settle their respawn timers when someone arrives. A cheap galaxy-wide event layer moves Swarm raids and roaming bosses between clusters. In co-op, players may split up, so the host simulates several spaces at once.
    Ship size and devicesNo fixed block cap. The drydock measures a design's simulation cost and warns when it's too heavy for the current device. Merged colliders and level of detail keep big hulls cheap.

    Spike, Bevy only · runs until every question has an answer

    Claude Code writes and runs it in your Linux VM (no GPU), in a local folder with local git (no remote repo yet). You watch it through the web build in your host browser; the APK goes to your flagship phone over Wi-Fi debugging. One block is 1 m.

    1. Physics benchmarkRapier2D, box2d-rust and Avian on the scenarios below: natively, on the phone and in the browser. The rest of the spike builds on the winner.
    2. CoreSim crate with ships as rigid bodies, grid damage and breakage; headless test that two runs give the same state hash.
    3. Web, Android + feelOne ship in the 2a look (atlas tiles, icons, hull edge, glow sprites; bloom on and off) as a web build and an APK. Stick + lock, WASD + mouse, 30 Hz sim with smooth 60 fps.
    4. Load test1, 5 and 20 ships with debris and projectiles. Phone pass: 60 fps using at most half the frame budget (about 8 ms per frame, sim step under 4 ms), plus temperature. Browser numbers are measured first; the web bar is set from them.
    5. Cross-device hashSame scenario natively, on the phone and in the browser; state hashes must match tick for tick.

    Tech and production
    Tech treesBig trees per family, about 200 nodes: parts, variants, size classes, refinements, alien branchesSettled
    BalancingAI vs AI arena on the headless simulation, with evolutionary search, a MAP-Elites archive and automatic number tuningSettled
    Tech stackAll Rust: engine-free sim crate, Bevy client for native and web (spike first), RON data. No C++, no Godot.Settled
    TargetsAndroid mid-range 2021+, Windows, Linux and the web as a full platform; 60 fps with a 30 Hz fixed simulation. Testing on a flagship means a headroom rule: at most half the frame budget at 20 shipsSettled
    DeterminismCross-platform, bit-exact: fixed step, seeded streams, ordered containers, software math, deterministic physics modeSettled
    NetcodeHost-authoritative snapshots with interpolation and prediction; join codes and a tiny relay; browser-friendly transport (WebRTC, WebSocket fallback)Settled
    ModsFrom the start: RON data plus Rhai scripts in a mods folder, same loader as the base game; in the browser from a zip or URLSettled
    SavesVersioned with migrations; export and import now, optional cloud sync later; browser storage on the webSettled
    Authoring and textHand-edited RON files, ships from drydock export; English only, all strings keyed in dataSettled
    Simulation spacesOne space per cluster, warp sequence between clusters; empty clusters freeze, a cheap event layer moves raids and roaming bosses; co-op players may split upSettled
    Ship size limitNo fixed cap; the drydock warns when a design is too heavy for the deviceSettled
    DevelopmentIn a Linux VM without GPU passthrough: native sim and tests, the game viewed as a web build in the host browser, Android via adb over Wi-FiSettled
    RepositoryClosed for now. No remote repos yet; the spike lives in a local folder with local git, written by Claude Code in the VM.Settled
    Physics enginePerformance decides, cross-platform determinism required. Benchmark Rapier2D, box2d-rust and Avian (C Box2D only as reference) as the first step of the spike; the spike builds on the winnerOpen
